About

The analyst who cares about the boring parts that make analysis trustworthy.

I'm a Data & Business Analyst currently at the University of Illinois, with earlier experience delivering regulated releases for a top-10 U.S. consumer lender at EXL. Across both, my work starts from a business question, not a dataset. On the hospital readmission project that meant asking where a care team should spend its limited follow-up hours; on Medicare Part D it meant which prescribers an audit team should open first, and why. The model is only the middle of the story — the ends are a clean question and an action someone can take.

I'm comfortable across the whole pipeline: pulling and cleaning genuinely messy data (97%-missing columns, sentinel ? values, records that must be excluded), expressing the logic in SQL that ports from DuckDB locally to Snowflake with an S3 landing zone, modeling in scikit-learn, and putting a Streamlit front end on top so the analysis doesn't die in a notebook.

I lean toward interpretable models and explicit guardrails — logistic regression with odds ratios over a black box when a care team needs the why; read-only, schema-scoped SQL when an LLM is allowed near a database. Trustworthy beats clever.

Medicare Part D Prescriber Anomaly Detection

Healthcare · Fraud / Integrity

Medicare Part D covers ~1M prescribers and tens of billions in drug spend; integrity teams can audit only a sliver. I score each prescriber against peers in their own specialty — robust median/MAD z-scores plus an IsolationForest — so an oncologist isn't compared to a family-practice NP. Every flag ships with a plain-English reason an auditor can act on.
