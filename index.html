01 FinTech · Retention

Who is actually worth a retention offer?

A card issuer loses about 1 in 6 cardholders a year. Predicting who will leave is the easy part. The real decision is who gets an offer, and when to stop.

What I built

A churn model scored out-of-fold, a 3-year value for every cardholder, and an offer ROI: churn probability × lifetime value × save rate, minus what the offer costs.

Try it

Move the sliders. This chart is computed live from all 10,000 customers in my project, ranked by expected value.

What it found

At a $100 offer and a 30% save rate, the best campaign targets the top ~4,200 customers (42%) for about $666K net. Past the peak, every extra offer costs more than it returns. ROC-AUC 0.83, top decile 3.6× the base rate.

Data: synthetic cardholders (10,000, 16.7% attrition) built on realistic disengagement signals. The pipeline also maps the public BankChurners dataset.

02 FinTech · Fraud

How deep should a fraud team review?

An investigations team can only look at so many alerts a day. My model ranks every transaction, so the question becomes how far down the list to go.

What I built

An interpretable fraud scorer that ranks transactions into an alert queue with a plain-English reason on each, judged on a held-out, most-recent time period.

Try it

Drag the review depth, then change what a review costs. The best cutoff moves, because net savings = fraud dollars caught minus review cost.

What it found

The top 1% of alerts is 62% fraud and recovers 52% of fraud dollars. At a $4 review cost the best cutoff reviews about 13% of alerts, catching about 80% of fraud cases and 97% of fraud dollars for roughly $458K net. ROC-AUC 0.913.

Data: schema-matched synthetic card transactions (220K, 2.8% fraud). The pipeline also maps a real public dataset into the same schema. Chart shows the held-out test period.

03 Marketing · Growth

Where should the next marketing dollar go?

A growth team spends about $580K across six channels. The answer is not "spend more". It is knowing which channels pay back and which lose money on every customer.

What I built

Channel-level CAC, LTV, LTV:CAC and payback across the acquisition funnel, plus a live budget-reallocation planner in the app.

Try it

Click a channel to see its funnel. Toggle the blended view to see how an average hides the losers.

What it found

Email 29×, Referral 20×, Organic 12×. Paid Search lands near 1.9×, while Paid Social (0.6×) and Display (0.2×) lose money on every customer. Cut Display, fix Paid Social, shift budget to the owned channels.

Did the new creative actually win?

A growth team tested a new ad creative on about 600K users. Three questions decide whether it ships: did it lift conversion, for whom, and how often should it run?

What I built

A two-proportion z-test with a confidence interval on the lift, a per-segment split, an exposure curve, and a guardrail check on unsubscribes.

Try it

Switch segments. The z-test, p-value and confidence interval recompute from the real user counts.

What it found

Conversion rose 3.07% to 3.43% (+11.5% relative, CI entirely above zero). Mobile drives the lift. The guardrail passes, so the decision is ship, mobile first, with a frequency cap.

04 Healthcare

Who comes back, and who deserves a closer look?

Hospitals are penalized for avoidable 30-day readmissions, but follow-up time is limited. Where should it go?

What I built

An interpretable logistic regression with odds ratios on about 101,766 real hospital encounters, plus a live patient risk scorer.

Try it

Switch the lens. Dashed line is the overall readmission rate for that view.

What it found

Prior utilization and discharge destination dominate. High-utilization patients sent to a SNF or rehab are readmitted at 16.2% vs a 7.4% baseline. The top risk decile captures 2.4× more readmits than random outreach. ROC-AUC 0.677.

Data: real UCI diabetes hospital-encounter dataset.

A flat threshold flags the wrong people.

Medicare Part D pays out tens of billions across about a million prescribers. Auditors can review a sliver. An oncologist's cost per claim dwarfs a family practitioner's, so one dollar cutoff cannot work.

What I built

Peer-relative scoring: each prescriber is compared only to their own specialty using robust median/MAD z-scores, plus an IsolationForest. The comparison also lives in SQL as a window-function view.

Try it

Switch between a flat threshold and peer-relative scoring, then slide the threshold. Points are an illustration; the method is the one in my project.

What it found

The flagged top 2% (300 prescribers) hold 6.5% of total drug spend. On planted anomalies the method recovers about 70% at about 63% precision in the top-k.

Project data: synthetic prescriber records (14,997) with known planted anomalies, so recovery can be measured.

05 GenAI · Text-to-SQL

Can you let an LLM near a database?

Analysts spend a big share of every week answering the same "can you pull" questions. A plain-English layer fixes that, but only if it can never change the data.

What I built

Ask-Your-Data: type a business question, the app drafts the SQL, validates it, runs it, charts the answer and explains it. The model sees the schema, never the rows, and the exact SQL is always shown.

Try it

Try to break the guardrail. Pick an attack or write your own query. It mirrors the rules in my app: one statement, a read-only SELECT, a row limit.

What it found

A free public demo with 16 curated questions that run with no API key. This is not an ML project: the real work is the data model, the SQL, and the governance.

Data: e-commerce star schema of customers, products, orders and order items.
